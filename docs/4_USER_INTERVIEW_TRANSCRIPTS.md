# User Qualitative Research Dossier: 3 In-Depth Interviews

**Target Segment:** Urban Young Working Professionals & Couples (Ages 23–30) in Tier-1 Metros  
**Research Methodology:** Semi-structured 30-minute qualitative interviews covering shopping habits, wardrobe frustrations, return frequency, and sizing challenges.

---

## Interview 1: Aditya Sharma
- **Age / Gender:** 26, Male
- **Occupation:** Senior Software Engineer (Fintech)
- **Location:** Indiranagar, Bengaluru
- **Living Situation:** Sharing apartment with friends; in a relationship.
- **Monthly Fashion Spend:** ₹6,000 – ₹12,000
- **Primary Apps Used:** Myntra, Amazon, Snitch, Zara

### Key Interview Excerpts & Observations:
1. **The Sizing Roulette:**
   > *"I have virtually given up on trusting an 'M' or 'L' label. When I buy t-shirts from Snitch, they fit super snug in the chest and armpits, so I have to order a Large. But when I order from Marks & Spencer or Zara, an 'L' looks like I borrowed my dad's shirt. Returning clothes in Bangalore is a massive friction point because delivery boys call you while you're in the middle of a sprint meeting."*
2. **The "Safe Color" Habit (Closet Bias):**
   > *"If you look inside my wardrobe right now, 70% of it is navy blue, black, or grey. Whenever I need a quick shirt for a weekend outing, I panic and buy another dark navy polo because I know it won't look terrible. But then I look at my photos from trips and every single photo looks identical."*
3. **Occasion Gaps & Footwear Disconnect:**
   > *"Last month we went to a family temple visit (Kemp Fort Shiva Temple) and later a traditional reception. I wore my navy short kurta with black pants, but I realized I didn't own any traditional footwear like Kolhapuris or ethnic sandals. I was literally wearing casual loafers that completely clashed with the look."*

### Persona Summary & Needs:
- Needs automated alerts when his wardrobe is overly biased towards one color spectrum.
- Desires pre-calculated sizes based on Indian brand tailoring.
- Wants immediate pairing suggestions for footwear when purchasing ethnic or smart-casual tops.

## Interview 2: Ananya Sen
- **Age / Gender:** 25, Female
- **Occupation:** Creative & Brand Strategist (Digital Media Agency)
- **Location:** Koramangala, Bengaluru
- **Living Situation:** Living independently in Bengaluru.
- **Monthly Fashion Spend:** ₹8,000 – ₹18,000
- **Primary Apps Used:** Myntra, Ajio Luxe, Zara, Mango, Instagram Shops

### Key Interview Excerpts & Observations:
1. **Instagram Inspiration vs. Commerce Friction:**
   > *"I spend hours on Instagram admiring how creators style Indo-Western looks—like taking a black Resham embroidered jacket and pairing it with wide-leg ecru trousers and chunky oxidized earrings. But when I go to Myntra, there is no way to search for that complete 'vibe'. I have to search for the jacket, then guess what bottoms won't look weird, then search for earrings separately. By the time I add everything to cart, half the items are out of stock in my size or the colors don't match in real life."*
2. **Bottom-Wear Anxiety for Petite Frames:**
   > *"I am 5'3\". Most pants online are photographed on 5'10\" models. I always stick to black high-waisted ankle trousers because they are safe and slimming. I would love to wear light ecru or pastel palazzos, but I'm terrified they will sweep the dusty Bangalore floor or look boxy around my waist."*
3. **Decision Fatigue & Cart Abandonment:**
   > *"I have 42 items in my Myntra wishlist right now. It's overwhelming. Sometimes I open the app to buy one cute top and after scrolling through 800 tiles for 45 minutes, I just close the app without buying anything."*

### Persona Summary & Needs:
- Needs 1-click Instagram aesthetic look cloning with auto-matched items from verified stores.
- Needs high-confidence sizing for non-black bottom wear tailored to petite Indian frames.
- Needs curated small-batch recommendations (3 items max) to eliminate wishlist fatigue.

## Interview 3: Rohan Varma
- **Age / Gender:** 28, Male
- **Occupation:** Senior Product Manager (Consumer Tech)
- **Location:** HSR Layout, Bengaluru
- **Living Situation:** Married; double-income household.
- **Monthly Fashion Spend:** ₹10,000 – ₹22,000
- **Primary Apps Used:** Myntra, Rare Rabbit, Uniqlo, FabIndia

### Key Interview Excerpts & Observations:
1. **The Cost of Blind E-Commerce Algorithms:**
   > *"E-commerce recommendations are fundamentally stupid. If I buy a running shoe on Amazon, it spends the next three weeks showing me 50 more running shoes. Why would I need another running shoe? What I actually need is moisture-wicking socks or running shorts that match the shoe! None of these apps look at what I actually own."*
2. **Fabric & Climate Awareness:**
   > *"I travel frequently between Bengaluru, Mumbai, and Delhi. In Bengaluru you need breathable layers for cool evenings, but in Mumbai heavy polyester makes you sweat instantly. Indian apps don't explain fabric weights or GSM in plain language. If an app told me: 'This linen shirt is 180 GSM slub, perfect for humid weather and replaces your faded plaid shirt', I would buy it on the spot."*
3. **Value of Time vs. Price Sensitivity:**
   > *"I don't care about a ₹200 discount coupon. What I care about is my time. If an app can guarantee that the shirt fits me on day one, matches my existing chinos, and eliminates the return cycle, I will gladly pay full price."*

### Persona Summary & Needs:
- High willingness to pay for cognitive ease and guaranteed fit.
- Rejection of repetitive recommendation algorithms; demands cross-category complements.
- Requires fabric transparency (GSM, slub, weave) suited to Indian climatic conditions.
